The mission

TeraLink-1 is a 6U CubeSat (about 10 × 20 × 30 cm) that aims to demonstrate the first sub-terahertz access link in low Earth orbit, with uplink and downlink at 220–225 GHz. Frequencies that high could carry far more data than today's satellite radios, but they have never been tested between space and the ground.

The satellite carries a custom sub-THz transceiver payload, and the other end of the link is a 2 m ground-station dish at Northeastern. It's built by the Northeastern Satellite Lab with Northeastern's Ultrabroadband Nanonetworking Laboratory (UN Lab) at the Institute for Intelligent Networked Systems, and funded through the Air Force Research Laboratory's University Nanosatellite Program.

The power system

The EPS keeps everything on the satellite alive. It has four jobs:

  • Harvest: triple-junction solar arrays on the outside of the spacecraft, with maximum-power-point tracking to get the most out of every orbit's sunlight.
  • Store: a lithium-ion battery pack that carries the satellite through eclipse and through high-power operations.
  • Distribute: regulated power rails, each switched on and off by the flight computer depending on the operating mode.
  • Protect: watchdogs, a low-battery safe mode that sheds non-essential loads, and launch inhibits that keep the satellite fully off until it's deployed.

The interesting challenge on TeraLink-1 is the payload. A terahertz experiment draws far more power than the solar arrays produce at any moment, so experiments have to run on stored energy, and the satellite recovers between them. Planning the operating modes around that energy balance is a core part of the EPS work.
